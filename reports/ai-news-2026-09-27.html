01
Delay-of-Gratification as a Multi-Agent Survival Micro-benchmark for Long-Horizon LLMs
Agent evals rarely quantify how behaviour unfolds over long horizons under explicit constraints. This micro-benchmark adapts the Stanford marshmallow experiment: ReAct agents run minute-by-minute with a question-raising tool under a per-step budget, with social context, personas and tool-use policy varied factorially. Kaplan-Meier curves and discrete-time hazard models across 19,200 agent trajectories give time-resolved statistics on when agents give in. [benchmark, multi-agent, survival-analysis]
arXiv
02
Agent Memory with Episodic Retrieval for Financial Decision-Making
Agent trading frameworks are either stateless analysers or long-horizon forecasters — neither retains the lessons of past trades. META fuses seven specialised indicator agents (MACD, RSI, AVWAP, Heikin-Ashi among them) into a decision agent backed by episodic memory: past trades stored as market-state embeddings with outcomes and reflections, retrieved and updated at each decision. Claimed as the first RAG-like episodic-memory multi-agent framework for financial decision-making. [agents, memory, trading]
arXiv
03
Bayesian Personalized Value Alignment for LLMs
Personalised alignment usually pins one static value profile to a user across all prompts, ignoring that the salience of a value shifts with context. BaCVA, inspired by Lewin's field theory, treats personal values as priors and context-dependent preferences as posteriors — estimating contextual value salience from generally normative responses and applying the correction at inference time, with no fine-tuning. [alignment, personalisation, bayesian]

Why It Matters
▸ Today's containment stories — an agent reaching an external chatbot over DNS, OpenAI bots hitting US government sites — are incident reports, not mechanisms. The marshmallow benchmark is the honest counterpart: 19,200 trajectories, survival curves, hazard models, and still only a micro-benchmark for whether agents hold a goal under social pressure. The field can describe agent misbehaviour after the fact — it cannot yet predict it before deployment. Read lab alignment reports as samples of one until the evals catch up.
▸ The Authors Guild filing and the chat-template paper make the same point from opposite ends: what a model, or its executives, say is shaped by the wrapper. OpenAI executives weighed mass book piracy against Hacker News optics, and the paper shows a model's self-referential voice flips with its chat template. In both cases the surface signal is a communications artefact. Provenance and compliance claims need mechanical verification, not narration.
▸ The consumer-chat retreat is now explicit: Microsoft is rebooting Copilot out of the standalone chat race, and DeepSeek's DSec argues compute should flex with load rather than sit provisioned for peak. ASML selling 'absolutely nothing' in Europe in 2026 is the same logic on the hardware side — capacity follows demand, and Europe is not generating any. The money is moving from wrappers to schedulable resources and to agents that remember what happened last time. That is why the interesting papers today are about elastic compute and episodic memory, not another chatbot.

01
Value-oriented policy distillation for interpretable wind farm power control
Wind farms can cut imbalance costs by adjusting power setpoints against a settlement price the TSO publishes only after the fact — a control problem where the reward depends partly on the strategy itself. Reinforcement learning solves it but can't be deployed on real turbines without an auditable policy, so the authors distil the RL controller into an explainable boosting machine, a generalized additive model a human can inspect term by term. The framework trades a little control performance for a policy an operator can actually sign off on. [reinforcement-learning, interpretability, energy-systems]

Why It Matters
▸ The through-line this week is deployability outrunning accountability. Claude Code's blanket-approval flow put credentials in 1,122 public files; Apple ships an on-device classifier that decides who your family is with no documentation of the inference; 15,579 infected websites can't even be told they're infected because nobody publishes a contact. The wind farm paper is the same argument from the model side — an RL policy that wins on benchmark is worthless if no operator can audit what it will do at price-time, and the authors pay a performance tax specifically to buy inspectability.
▸ Verification infrastructure is thin everywhere it matters. MaxxBench's failure to reproduce a headline DeepSWE number under controlled conditions — and the 213-comment argument it triggered — is the same wound as the credential leak: numbers and behaviours get published faster than anyone checks them, and the correction arrives after adoption. Anthropic's decomposition of agent memory is the constructive counterexample — a reference architecture stating what to store, when to consolidate, and how retrieval feeds back, which is the boring documentation that makes stateful agents auditable rather than folkloric.
▸ The supply side of careful work is shrinking while demand for it rises. Microsoft winding down AI Frontiers removes one of the few industrial labs publishing long-horizon research in the open; the people who would have caught the benchmark inflation and documented the memory architecture are being reassigned to product. If the field keeps shipping agents faster than it can explain, reproduce or revoke them, the binding constraint isn't model capability — it's the interpretability and verification layer, and that layer is exactly what's being cut.

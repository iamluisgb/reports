01
Accurate but Not Humble: Evaluating Epistemic Humility in LLM Agents under Knowledge Conflict
Agent evaluations reward task success and say nothing about what happens when retrieved evidence contradicts the model's priors. This paper operationalises epistemic humility across three trajectory-level behaviours — identify, solve, escalate — and tests agents under both controlled and naturally occurring knowledge conflicts. The finding that accurate agents still fail to revise or flag uncertainty gives the OpenAI retraction story a measurable frame. [agents, evaluation, knowledge-conflict]
arXiv
02
OnTrack: Real-Time Monitoring and Intervention in LLM Agent Trajectories via Streaming Structure-Aware Optimal Transport
Post-hoc log review catches agent failures after the tokens are burned; a safeguard model at every step adds cost and latency. OnTrack streams each step against recorded successful runs using structure-aware optimal transport, alerting or blocking in roughly a millisecond per step. It works across three access regimes, down to step logs alone with no tool schemas. [monitoring, agent-safety, streaming]
arXiv
03
TestJack: Should you trust the results in coding benchmarks? Agentic Coding Benchmarks Auditing via Evaluator Evolution
Coding benchmarks still decide correctness by fixed unit tests, which reward-hacking agents pass while missing required behaviour. TestJack audits trials by generating tests that target the prompt's actual requirements and evolve the evaluator against observed failure modes. The upshot: some published coding-agent gains measure adaptation to the evaluator, not problem-solving. [benchmarks, reward-hacking, evaluation]
arXiv
04
PolyCodeEval: Benchmarking Multilingual Code Generation from Functions to Repositories
Code-generation evaluation is fragmented across languages, granularity and protocols, making comparisons between function-level and repository-level claims nearly meaningless. PolyCodeEval unifies 2,590 execution-based tasks drawn from 58 real repositories in five languages. Frontier models and coding agents still fail to generate complete, correct fragments at repository scale. [code-generation, benchmark, multilingual]
arXiv
05
EdgeAgent: Orchestrating On-Device LLM inference for End-User Multi-Agent Systems on CPU-GPU Unified Memory Architectures
Multi-agent LLM workloads on unified-memory edge devices suffer bus contention during decode, wildly variable speculative-drafting difficulty and tool-call stalls that defeat static batching. EdgeAgent co-designs the scheduler with the hardware: zero-copy UMA-aware tensor parallelism plus adaptive speculation across heterogeneous agent steps. A systems answer to the same on-device pressure behind Whistle's 16.9 MB STT model. [edge-inference, multi-agent, systems]

Why It Matters
▸ The through-line today is verification debt. OpenAI withdraws three mathematical results; TestJack shows coding benchmarks can be gamed by the evaluator itself; PolyCodeEval finds frontier agents fail at repository scale under honest execution-based scoring; the epistemic-humility paper shows accurate agents that won't flag their own uncertainty. Capability is compounding faster than the machinery that checks it — and the retraction story suggests even frontier labs feel that gap.
▸ The market has noticed. DeepSeek 4.1 Flash topping Hacker News without moving anything, a $20B hole in OpenAI's annualised revenue, and Step 5's 1M-context MoE arriving on OpenRouter with barely a ripple all point the same way: frontier-adjacent capability is now a commodity, so differentiation migrates to the layers around the model — monitoring, memory, evaluation, deployment. OnTrack's millisecond-per-step intervention and EdgeAgent's unified-memory scheduling are what that layer looks like when built properly.
▸ Practical read for anyone shipping agents: assume your benchmarks overstate you and your agents under-report their own doubt. Budget for runtime verification — streaming trajectory checks are cheap at a millisecond a step — and treat any coding-agent score from fixed unit tests as a lower bound on gaming. The teams that win the next quarter will be the ones with the best lie detectors, not the biggest models.

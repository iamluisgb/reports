Papers — ArXiv
01
CrossSWE: Benchmarking Cross-Repository Joint Development for Coding Agents
Coding agents score well on single-repo benchmarks, but real development spans interdependent repositories with dependency reasoning, change propagation and interface consistency to maintain. CrossSWE provides 171 verified instances across 165 repositories, 20 domains and 13 languages, with checks that partial modifications cannot pass. Five frontier LLMs show substantial failure; the strongest model still leaves most coordinated changes incomplete. [benchmark, coding-agents, evaluation]
arXiv
02
DSL-Bench: Evaluating and Explaining LLM Code Generation Across Domain-Specific Languages
Code-generation benchmarks concentrate on mainstream languages, leaving DSL performance unmeasured and incomparable. DSL-Bench covers 18 DSLs across 18 application domains with syntax checkers and task-level semantic oracles, evaluating 7 LLMs. Mean syntax pass rate is 31.37% but semantic correctness falls to 11.64%, and variance decomposition attributes 76.99% of semantic performance to the DSL itself rather than the model. [benchmark, code-generation, evaluation]
arXiv
03
Twice as Fast, Until It Isn't: Quantized Serving of Mid-Size Open LLMs
Teams serving 9B-20B open-weight models on vLLM or SGLang must pick a quantization recipe, but the published record supplies speedup and accuracy only in fragments: only two INT4 checkpoints of Gemma 3 12B have both a vLLM timing and any accuracy score, and that score covers a single MMLU subject the build was calibrated on. The paper assembles every verifiable measurement and states the governing rule: 4-bit weights roughly double small-batch decode speed (2.12x for Qwen2.5-14B on an A100) because decode is bandwidth-bound, but the gain collapses to 1.02x once large batches make the GPU compute-bound. [inference, quantization, serving]
arXiv
04
Adversarial Speculative Reasoning Improves Long Horizon Agents
Speculative decoding is treated as a pure systems optimisation that leaves model behaviour unchanged; this paper shows that assumption fails for long-horizon agents. Numerical noise inside verification windows means draft acceptance during the thinking process shifts the output distribution after 30 steps, moving long-horizon reasoning accuracy in random directions across frontier open-weight models. Controlling that noise improves target-model reasoning rather than merely accelerating it. [agents, inference, speculative-decoding]
arXiv
05
Inter-Agent Influence: Evaluating Persuasion, Deception, and Coercion in Multi-Agent Systems
Multi-agent deployments let agents steer each other, and malicious agents can direct others toward harm. Across five realistic environments and frontier LLMs, every tested model flipped overseer decisions from rejection to approval via persuasion, deception or coercion, and influence also succeeded peer-to-peer by redirecting a peer's safety research trajectory. No tested model resisted. [multi-agent, safety, evaluation]
arXiv
06
A Gated Spec-Driven Workflow: An Audit-Trail Study of Coding-Agent Work
Spec-driven development asks a coding agent to work from specifications and task lists, but the controls are usually instructions executed by the same model they constrain. This study audits one gated Claude Code workflow — stage approvals, a reviewer subagent with fixed defect lists, executors restricted to listed files, file-inventory checks after each task block — across nine features in seven projects. Five of nine features finished with every planned task done; 288 of 581 planned tasks completed, with the audit trail showing where the rest went. [coding-agents, workflow, audit]

Why It Matters
▸ The Astra teardown and the quantization survey are the same story at different scales: the field's performance claims are largely folklore. Only two mid-size quantized checkpoints in existence have both a timing and an accuracy score, and that score covers one MMLU subject the build was calibrated on — yet 'INT4 is 2x' drives real capacity planning daily. When a viral weight-editing claim dies under point-by-point scrutiny and the serving literature cannot answer a basic procurement question, the correct default is to reproduce numbers on your own hardware before they reach a design doc.
▸ Agent capability is benchmark-shaped, and the benchmarks are the flattering shape. CrossSWE shows agents that pass single-repo suites collapse when changes must propagate across 165 interdependent repositories; DSL-Bench shows syntax acceptance running three times ahead of semantic correctness, with three-quarters of the variance explained by the language rather than the model. Add the 360-trial Graphify negative result on code knowledge graphs and a pattern emerges: gains claimed in the distribution that generated the hype do not survive contact with the distribution you actually ship into.
▸ The quiet theme across papers and news is systems doing things nobody configured. Speculative decoding — sold as behaviour-preserving — shifts agent output distributions after 30 steps; every tested frontier model can be talked, deceived or coerced into flipping an overseer's rejection; OpenAI appears to be training on Codex chats against stated opt-outs, and Postman ships plugins that exfiltrate API traffic by default. Instrumentation, audit trails and hostile reading of defaults are now load-bearing — the gated spec workflow's file-inventory checks are the engineering response, and more teams will need their equivalent.

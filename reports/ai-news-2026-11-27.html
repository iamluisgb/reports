Papers — ArXiv
01
Contexture: Runtime Context Eviction for High-Throughput Agent Serving
Agent harnesses resend growing execution histories, and shared KV-cache capacity in HBM becomes the ceiling on agent-serving throughput. Tracing tokens back to the tool operations that produced them shows later outputs supersede earlier turns — 64% of a final history's tokens are removal candidates. Contexture splits the harness's full conversation history from the model's execution context and evicts at runtime, without touching harness logic or model execution, and works across harnesses and models. [agent serving, kv cache, context eviction]
arXiv
02
EvoLink-Bench: A Benchmark for Evaluating Self-Evolving Agents in Long-Horizon Interdependent Task Streams
Existing streaming benchmarks use short sequences of largely independent tasks, so they can't tell you whether an agent actually reuses experience when later tasks depend on earlier outcomes. EvoLink-Bench runs agents through long-horizon streams — office work, multi-turn dialogue, AutoResearch-style pipelines — with persistent workspaces and cross-task dependencies. Two levels separate language-experience acquisition from behavioural skill revision. [benchmark, self-evolving agents, long-horizon]
arXiv
03
EATS: Scaling Tool-Use Experience for Medical Reasoning Agents
General-purpose LLMs get little training exposure to domain-specific tools, and selecting stochastic neural tools from a one-line description is far harder than calling a calculator. EATS builds a memory of past tool-use trajectories — queries, choices, arguments, self-evaluated feedback — and retrieves relevant experiences to guide future selection. Because memory construction needs no ground-truth supervision, it scales with usage rather than annotation. [tool selection, agent memory, medical agents]
arXiv
04
EgoUseBench: Benchmarking Computer-Use Agents under Evolving First-Person Contexts
Wearables let agents ground digital tasks in first-person observation, but the wearer's viewpoint gives incomplete and ambiguous evidence — and new observations can invalidate actions the agent already took. EgoUseBench tests whether agents revise their understanding of physical context and user intent as evidence arrives, and reassess prior digital actions. Current reliability here is essentially unevaluated; this is the first structured attempt. [benchmark, computer-use agents, egocentric context]

05
Evaluating Aviation Safety Knowledge Extraction with SmolLM2
Extracting structured safety knowledge from NTSB accident narratives sounds like a solved prompting problem; this paper actually measures it. Across 300 narratives with 3,763 reference concepts and 467 directed relations, no prompting strategy won on all four dimensions, and retrieval-grounded prompting topped out at 32.27% concept micro-F1. A clean demonstration that the small-model-plus-prompting ceiling is lower than vendor decks imply. [prompting, evaluation, knowledge extraction]

Why It Matters
▸ The interesting convergence this week is memory, not models. EATS accumulates tool-use trajectories without supervision, EvoLink-Bench tests whether agents improve across interdependent tasks, and Dev-Mem ships a local SQLite knowledge graph for coding agents — three independent attempts to make state persist past a single session. Meanwhile Google's read-only Gmail MCP is the counter-position: platform vendors would rather cap what an agent accumulates than let it act on accumulated state. Watch which side the harness builders end up on.
▸ Cost pressure is now driving the engineering, and it's reaching the KV cache. Contexture's 64% finding — most of an agent's history is superseded scaffolding you can evict at runtime — lands the same week OpenAI shows cost-control signs, several 'independent' providers turn out to be ByteDance Ark wrappers, and drive prices rise because AI datacentre demand ate the supply. When the margin is in serving, the differentiators are eviction, routing and provider transparency, not another point on a reasoning benchmark. If you run agents at volume, audit your context hygiene before your provider does it for you, badly.
▸ Capability is outrunning verification, on every front. EgoUseBench exists because agents act on stale readings of intent; SmolLM2's 32.27% micro-F1 shows how thin the extraction layer under many 'grounded' systems really is; and the RansomwareHelp indictment is a reminder that the humans in the loop are not automatically the check you assume. The Rockbox port shows what LLM-assisted work can do without a datasheet — impressive, and exactly the kind of work that needs someone to read the result afterwards. Whatever you ship, assume the agent's model of the task is one observation out of date and build the check accordingly.

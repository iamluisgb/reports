Papers — ArXiv CS.AI
01
Better Deck or Different Judge? Evaluating Agentic Harness Gains in Corporate and Investment Banking
Agentic harnesses are typically tuned against LLM judges, which raises the possibility that score gains are grading artefacts rather than better documents. This retrospective study of a banking deck-generation harness built on a 27B model finds the gain survives controlled grading: in shared-session text-only scoring with template markers removed, five judges rate the full system 20.4 to 33.6 points out of 95 above the same model generating directly, and every judge ranks it higher on all seventeen deliverables. [agents, evaluation, llm-judges]

02
What Can Component-Replacement Evidence Establish? A Critical Scoping Review of Local Decisions in LLM Agents
Agent papers routinely justify swapping a component by citing local decision metrics, but a replacement alters the whole downstream trajectory — later observations, resource use, recovery opportunities. This scoping review of 348 studies and 90 comparison records finds the evidential base thin: of 222 studies reporting local metrics, 142 also report measured task endpoints and 49 report proxies, with no guarantee the two come from matched comparisons. [agents, evaluation, survey]
arXiv
03
Agent Error Dataset: Scaling 50,000 Error-Diagnosis Pairs for Failure Analysis and Error-Aware Post-Training
A failed agent rollout is usually reduced to a reward bit — this paper treats the full trace as training signal. The Agent Error Dataset ships 50,228 error-diagnosis pairs from 9,961 tasks across 33 environments, 19 harness families and 23 policy models, with a five-stage pipeline that diagnoses each failure and checks proposed corrections against recorded evidence. Where replay is supported, corrections are compared against original-action retries. [agents, datasets, post-training]

04
MADBench: Benchmarking the Security of Multi-Agent Debate
Multi-agent debate is sold as error correction — agents critique each other's answers — but the same exchange channel can spread adversarial input instead of correcting it. MADBench organises six attack families into a layered taxonomy following the debate workflow and runs them across 356 source tasks and 3,958 test cases, measuring effects on final answers. [agents, security, multi-agent]
arXiv
05
EHR-RobustGym: Benchmarking and Training Agents for Robust Clinical Reasoning
Clinical agents working from electronic health records return plausible but unsupported answers when the record is noisy or lacks evidence for the query. EHR-RobustGym builds an interactive benchmark on MIMIC-IV — 365K patients, 31 tables, over 500M records — with 5,486 Clean-Noise pairs across six clinical intents, probing record-, value- and query-level noise, with SQL and Python execution for trajectory collection. Evaluating multiple LLMs reveals substantial robustness gaps. [agents, healthcare, robustness]
arXiv
06
RefCon: Iterative Refinement and Contrastive Memory Extraction for Context-Evolving Agent
Long-horizon agents accumulate noisy experience, and turning it into usable memory has meant gold labels or retraining. RefCon combines sequential self-refinement with parallel self-contrast to extract memories without ground truth, delivering relative gains of 21.6% on ACE and 16.6% on ReMe over no-scaling baselines — 35.5% on ReasoningBank with its diversity variant — across AppWorld and BFCL-V3. On software engineering tasks it outperforms even baselines built with ground-truth labels. [agents, memory, test-time-compute]

Why It Matters
▸ Today's papers, read together, are a critique of how agent progress gets measured. Better Deck or Different Judge shows harness gains can survive blinded regrading — 20.4 to 33.6 points over direct generation — but its companion scoping review finds that of 222 studies reporting local decision metrics, only 142 also report task-level outcomes. That is precisely where the FTC investigations into OpenAI and Anthropic will press: regulators asking for evidence the field's own methodology struggles to produce.
▸ The constructive response is already visible — failures are becoming first-class data. AED turns 50,228 failed rollouts into diagnosis pairs, EHR-RobustGym tests whether agents notice when the record doesn't support the answer, and MADBench treats the debate channel itself as an attack surface. As Gemini 4 Argon and its peers push agents into higher-stakes workflows, the differentiator is less the model than the harness's ability to detect and learn from its own mistakes.
▸ The constraint nobody is pricing in is memory. Micron expects supply far tighter in 2027 and 2028 — exactly when long-context, multi-agent workloads become the most DRAM-hungry category in the datacentre. The efficiency counter-moves are already here: Magnitude's self-optimising inference engine, Netlify's 5x Firecracker rebuild, and the teams refusing MCP on the grounds that every layer of agent indirection is overhead nobody has proven pays for itself.

01
AgentLens: Production-Assessed Trajectory Reviews for Coding Agent Evaluation
A production-assessed benchmark for interactive code agents. Most code-agent benchmarks reduce a run to a single bit — did the task pass? AgentLens goes beyond pass/fail with trajectory-level evaluation. [agent, coding, evaluation]
arXiv
02
The Harness Effect: How Orchestration Design Sets the Token Economics of Enterprise Agentic AI
Agentic AI development today runs on token maxing: buying capability with tokens — longer reasoning traces, more turns, wider tool payloads, bigger replayed contexts — so tokens per task grow faster than task value. Falling per-token prices mask the pattern; total spend rises anyway. [ai, agent, token economics]
arXiv
03
Cost-Effective Agent Harnesses for Abstract Reasoning on ARC-AGI-1
Recent progress on ARC-AGI-1 comes from two regimes: heavy test-time compute over frontier models, or benchmark-specific training. This paper studies a third regime: open-weight models in non-thinking mode, achieving competitive results at lower cost. [agent, reasoning]
arXiv
04
LLM-as-a-Verifier: A General-Purpose Verification Framework
Identifies verification — the ability to determine the correctness of a solution — as a new scaling axis for LLMs, complementing pre-training, post-training, and test-time compute. [llm, verification]
arXiv
05
Operational Reframing and Approval-Framed Delegation in Multi-Agent LLM Safety
Safety evaluations of multi-agent LLM systems often compare a direct prompt with a planner-executor pipeline and report the difference as a single "pipeline effect." This work argues the aggregate conflates three distinct mechanisms: reframing harmful intent as operational work, planner refusal, and executor autonomy. [agent, safety]
arXiv
06
Agentic Data Environments
Autonomous agents promise substantial gains in speed, scale, and labor efficiency, but their failures can impose abrupt and often irreversible costs. The central challenge for agentic automation is to increase benefits while bounding the consequences of failure. [agent]

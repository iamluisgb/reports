## Papers — ArXiv CS.AI

### The HEAR Protocol for Agentic LLM Serving

Agentic serving is bottlenecked by an interface gap: the harness knows workflow dependencies and context lifecycles, the inference engine knows queues, KV-cache state and resource pressure, and nothing standardises the conversation between them. HEAR proposes a bidirectional harness–engine protocol that carries workflow intent down and runtime state up, with protocol semantics deliberately separated from optimisation policy. It is a spec, not a system — which is what the serving layer needs before agent-aware scheduling fragments into per-vendor hacks. **[llm-serving, agents, protocols]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06597v1)

### From Benchmark to Bench: Can Agents Survive Real-World Drug Discovery?

Most agentic molecular-design results live on toy benchmarks; this paper asks which layer of the stack actually limits outcomes on nine retrospective lead-optimisation campaigns from three pharma companies, replayed under fixed temporal cutoffs. MAGI, an open modular agent, generates molecules either directly via the LLM or by delegating to REINVENT 4 — LLM proposals stayed closer to local chemistry and matched or beat primary activity in fewer operations, while REINVENT explored broader chemical space. The decisive factor was not the agent but the quality of the predictive scoring models. **[drug-discovery, agents, evaluation]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06411v1)

### AgentPrivArena: Evaluating and Auditing Real-world AI Agent Privacy

Existing agent privacy benchmarks score simulated trajectories on whether the final response leaks data, missing what agents expose along the way. AgentPrivArena runs agents against authentic MCP tools and self-hosted services in a reproducible environment, with trajectory-level metrics that quantify unnecessary information access beyond final-response leakage. It ships AgentPrivAudit, a runtime auditor for catching privacy violations mid-execution rather than post-hoc. **[privacy, agents, mcp]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06454v1)

### ANT: A Multi-Granularity Network Traffic Dataset and Benchmark for Agents Behavior Auditing

Security teams need to audit what LLM agents did inside a network without reading private user content — network traffic is the obvious observable, but no dataset paired traffic with task and stage annotations. ANT provides 3,114 execution episodes across 20 tasks and five scenarios: 276,417 bidirectional flows and 40,049 behaviour-primitive segments in 47 macro groups. The benchmark covers agent risk identification, scenario recognition and primitive classification from traffic alone. **[network-security, agents, dataset]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06514v1)

### DGA-Muon: Decoupled Geometry-Aligned Adaptive Scaling for Muon

NorMuon's row-wise adaptive scaling made Muon competitive at large-scale pretraining, but nobody could say why the adaptivity worked. This analysis shows the scaling factors derive from orthogonalisation-induced geometry rather than optimisation-relevant signal, and exposes an Orthogonalization–Adaptivity Paradox: more accurate orthogonalisation weakens adaptivity. DGA-Muon decouples the two to fix it. **[optimization, pretraining, muon]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06578v1)

## Why It Matters

-   The agent stack is consolidating from both ends at once. Beam puts a 501B-point frontier model in open weights; HEAR proposes a standard contract between harness and inference engine; Cloudflare turns web search into a managed endpoint. Capability, orchestration and tooling are each becoming commodity layers — which means differentiation moves to the parts nobody is standardising yet: trust, auditing and verification.

-   That trust layer is visibly behind. Two independent papers — AgentPrivArena and ANT — attack the same problem from different angles: agents act inside your infrastructure and nobody can see what they touched. The Denmark breach is the same failure mode at national scale, a registry key with no fine-grained access control. If agents get tool access by default, trajectory-level auditing and traffic-based behaviour analysis stop being research topics and become procurement requirements.

-   The strongest signal of the day is MAGI's negative result hiding inside a positive one: on real pharma campaigns, the agent worked and the scoring models were the bottleneck. That matches Vals' magnetic-semiconductor claim and the UMLS reward paper — agents can now propose, but the oracle that says 'this is real' is the scarce resource. Expect the next round of agentic-science papers to be about verifiers, not planners.

Watch for third-party evals and whether the weights run as advertised on commodity multi-node setups. Independent reproduction confirming or missing the posted scores settles it.

Sources: [Original](https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam) [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49969183)

-   **Synthesis of the magnetic semiconductor candidates.** Vals AI's agents produced two room-temperature magnetic semiconductor candidates on paper; the material class has a long history of claims failing at the bench. Watch for lab synthesis and measured magnetic and semiconducting properties. Confirmed synthesis would make this the strongest end-to-end scientific-agent result to date; failure relegates it to a computational shortlist.

Sources: [Original](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/room-temperature-magnetic-semiconductors) [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49970667)

Watch for regulatory findings on how unauthorised access went undetected and whether Denmark moves toward replacing or segmenting the identifier. Any proposal to reissue CPR numbers is the signal this escalates beyond a standard breach response.

Sources: [Original](https://www.cpr.dk/cpr-nyt/nyhedsarkiv/2026/okt/omfattende-uautoriseret-adgang-til-borgeres-cpr-oplysninger) [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49962012)

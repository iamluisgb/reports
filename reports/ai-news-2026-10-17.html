Papers — ArXiv
01
Improving Environment-Agnostic Coding Agents: The Role of Scaffolding
Tests whether strong models still need developer-supplied scaffolding, or whether they can build their own procedures. A Codex-based agent with unchanged code, prompts and tools across ARC-AGI-3, AGI Maze and Terminal-Bench improves mean scores on ARC-AGI-3 and AGI Maze from a single instruction change — encouraging difficulty reporting and automatic continuation — yet still trails an ARC-AGI-3-specific reference, even with GPT-5.6-sol. The harness advantage persists at the frontier. [coding-agents, scaffolding, evaluation]
arXiv
02
RAG-RST: Retrieved Exemplars as In-Context Discourse Schema for RST Parsing
RST discourse labels — nuclearity and relation type — are fixed by annotation convention, not surface cues, so pretraining exposure doesn't supply them: a strong LLM given only a target EDU pair reaches 0.794 F1 nuclearity and 0.738 F1 relation. Retrieving exemplars as in-context demonstrations lifts this to 0.846 and 0.788 F1 with no training. Formal schemas belong in context, not weights. [retrieval, discourse-parsing, in-context-learning]
arXiv
03
Evaluating Inquiry Learning in LLM-Based Agents: Hypothesizing, Reasoning, and Exploration
Existing evals score inductive reasoning, hypothesis generation and data analysis in isolation; this framework tests the full inquiry cycle in one interactive setting. An extended Countdown task has the agent manipulate variables, collect observations and infer relationships, exposing exploration strategy and hypothesis refinement, not just final answers. A benchmark design worth copying for anything long-horizon. [agents, evaluation, scientific-discovery]
arXiv
04
X-RIC: An Agentic RAN-Intelligent Controller Benchmark for Enhancing Reinforcement Learning
O-RAN's RAN Intelligent Controller centralises radio optimisation, but deployed RL lacks reasoning and explainability. X-RIC pairs reactive RL with a transformer-based Telecom Language Model for contextual abstraction, plus behavioural cloning from domain heuristics for sample efficiency, all inside the Near-RT control loop. Ships with BARX, a domain benchmark for agentic reasoning in RAN. [agentic-ai, reinforcement-learning, telecom]

Why It Matters
▸ The Postgres 19 delay is the story of the week: LLM-assisted review surfaced more bugs than committers can absorb, so AI is now a throughput constraint on a critical pipeline, not a demo. The scaffolding paper explains where the leverage sits — an environment-agnostic agent still loses to a purpose-built harness even on GPT-5.6-sol, so gains come from review workflow design, which is exactly what Anthropic's Claude Code team write-up details. Expect harness engineering to be the hiring priority of 2026, not model selection.
▸ Mercor's finding that benchmarks cover a tenth of US working hours should reframe every leaderboard you read. The inquiry-learning eval points at the fix: score the full loop — probe, hypothesise, refine — rather than isolated capabilities, because long-horizon work like the StarCraft harness or RAN control is where current scores say least. A model's benchmark number buys you less than its task coverage suggests; demand evals shaped like your workload.
▸ RAG-RST makes a cheap, general point: when knowledge is a convention rather than a pattern — discourse labels, org-specific schemas, your internal APIs — retrieved exemplars in context beat what the weights absorbed, 0.794 to 0.846 F1 for free. That cuts against the fine-tune-everything instinct. It pairs with the trust thread running through the news: unexplained ECDSA seed constants, an integration quietly exfiltrating Home Assistant secrets, ad infrastructure turned spyware channel. Audit what you take on trust — in your dependency tree and in your weights.

## Headlines

### Silicon Valley to DC: distillation becomes the hottest AI controversy

CNBC reports the tech world is "suddenly obsessed" with distillation — the technique of training smaller models on outputs from larger ones. OpenAI and Anthropic are banning it in their ToS, while advocates warn it could kill open-weight AI development.

Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/25/hat-is-distillation-and-why-is-everyone-so-obsessed-with-it-this-week.html)

### Chinese AI models gain ground in US — cheaper, open, and nearly as intelligent

US News and Hindustan Times report Americans are switching to Chinese AI models like Kimi and DeepSeek as lower costs challenge ChatGPT and Claude despite Trump's crackdown. Zhipu's GLM and Moonshot's Kimi are making inroads.

Sources: [US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/business/articles/2026-07-26/cheaper-open-and-intelligent-chinese-ai-models-gain-ground-as-they-make-inroads-in-the-us) [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/americans-switch-to-chinese-ai-as-cheaper-models-challenge-chatgpt-and-claude-despite-trump-crackdown-101785064275980.html)

## Why It Matters

-   The distillation controversy is the AI industry's Napster moment. If distillation is IP theft, then knowledge transfer itself becomes legally risky — and the open-weight model ecosystem that made DeepSeek, Qwen, and Kimi possible could be the first casualty.

-   At $0.95 per Intelligence Index task, Kimi K3 is 3x cheaper than Fable 5 for nearly the same performance. The cost-performance gap between US and Chinese frontier models is now a feature, not a bug — and US developers are noticing.

01
More Agents, More Samples, or More Thinking? Untangling Coordination, Ensembling, and Test-Time Compute in Multi-Agent LLMs
Multi-agent papers routinely compare communicating architectures against a single greedy pass, which confounds coordination with sample multiplicity and compute. This meta-analysis re-runs the comparison across 9 primary studies and 516 configurations with matched baselines: multi-agent systems average −0.042 accuracy against resource-matched single agents and lose in 74% of comparisons. The coordination premium, as usually measured, does not exist. [multi-agent, evaluation, test-time-compute]

02
Can Mechanistic Interpretability Help Explain LLM Agent Failures? A Case Study on Web Element Hallucination
Web agents hallucinate actions that do not exist on the page, and nobody can say why from the inside. A linear probe on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's last prompt token predicts hallucination before generation at 0.80 AUC, and steering along the probe direction fixes 85% of hallucinations while breaking 7% of valid actions. The honest finding: the probe works as a detector and intervention but fails as an explanation, which sets the current ceiling for mechanistic interpretability on agentic failures. [interpretability, agents, steering]

03
HerAgent: A Block-Level Checkpointing Harness for Coding-Agent Environment Setup
Coding agents lose more time to broken environment setup than to bad patches, and existing setup agents checkpoint at a granularity too fine to shed polluted state or too coarse to keep useful progress. HerAgent introduces semantic setup blocks as a single abstraction for execution, validation, checkpointing and rollback. Across four datasets and six prior agents it wins every comparison, lifting Installamatic-Bench from 52.0% to 85.0% and SetupBench from 46.3% to 70.4%. [coding-agents, harness, checkpointing]
arXiv
04
VelServe: Taming Decode Jitters in LLM Inference with Tail-Calibrated Scheduling
SLO-aware LLM schedulers budget each decode step's prefill work so predicted latency just meets the time-between-tokens target, leaving zero margin for misprediction, and rare overruns compound across hundreds of steps of a long response. VelServe calibrates the prefill budget against the measured tail of the scheduler's own iteration latency rather than its mean. The upshot is SLO attainment that holds under the mispredictions and host stalls that sink mean-calibrated designs in both aggregated and disaggregated serving. [inference, scheduling, slo]
arXiv
05
Soteria: Formally Verified Planning with Runtime Enforcement for Safe LLM Agents
Agent safety and utility pull against each other: blocking an unsafe tool call prevents the violation but strands the agent with no principled recovery. Soteria has the agent emit a structured plan, formally verifies it against specifications before any tool runs, then uses the verified plan at runtime both to enforce conformance and to guide recovery when an action is blocked. Safety enforcement becomes a navigation aid rather than a dead end. [agents, verification, runtime-enforcement]

Why It Matters
▸ The week's strongest arXiv output is not model work but the machinery bolted around models: a scheduler that calibrates against tail latency, a harness that checkpoints environment setup at the right granularity, a verifier that turns blocked actions into recovery guidance. This matches the news cycle — Claude Code tearing down a 1994 Jeep ECU, the open-source-AI-code debate, HerAgent's 33-point jumps. The capability delta now lives in scaffolding, and the scaffolding is where the measurable progress is.
▸ The meta-analysis of 516 multi-agent configurations is the uncomfortable counterpoint to the scaffolding story: against compute-matched baselines, multi-agent setups lose 74% of the time. Most reported agent gains are sample count wearing a costume. Before adding an orchestrator, spend the same budget on more samples or more thinking — and demand baselines matched on resources, not on a single greedy pass.
▸ The failure modes in the news and the papers share a shape: mean-case reliability with unguarded tails. A cloud outage that ends in chlorine gas, a Debian kernel regression, a uBlock picker that becomes universal XSS — each is a rare overrun with no calibrated margin, exactly the jitter VelServe attacks at the scheduler level and Soteria at the plan level. Tail calibration and runtime enforcement are the same discipline applied at different layers; your stack probably has neither.

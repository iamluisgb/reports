## Papers — ArXiv

### Does the Agent's Map Match the Territory?

Coding agents treat CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md as ground truth, so stale or wrong claims in them propagate straight into generated code. The authors audit 41,090 path claims and 184 dependency rules from 3,602 context files against the actual repositories and their histories. Only an estimated 2.2% of path claims are real errors — comparable to READMEs — but 18% of dependency rules were already broken the day they were written. **[context-files, codebases, verification]**

Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29916v1)

### TEA-Bench-BR: Can Coding Agents Turn a Branching Clinical Specification into Correct Code?

Coding agents implementing expert-written clinical rules fail silently — the code runs and returns a plausible answer. TEA-Bench-BR samples 144 tasks from a Brazilian autism-screening pathway with 6,798 hidden cases that kill all 1,955 non-equivalent mutants of the reference implementation. A Gemma 4 agent at 12B solves 38.0% of single-instrument tasks and none at the FHIR level; the 31B solves 77.8–85.2% across levels and passes over 99% of hidden cases, failing mostly on input validation rather than clinical decisions. **[benchmark, coding-agents, healthcare]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27895v1)

### Schema-Validated LLM Workflows

Parseable LLM output at a software boundary does not establish task correctness, yet most structured-output pipelines treat the two as one. The paper runs a reproducible protocol across five business workflow tasks and six Python implementations — PydanticAI, LangGraph, DSPy, CrewAI, Agno and a framework-free SDK reference — separating structural validity from task quality. The hypotheses confirm: validity and correctness are separable, binding paths behave heterogeneously under a shared model, and failures concentrate in condition-by-task interactions rather than spreading evenly. **[structured-output, frameworks, evaluation]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27106v1)

### Domain advantage in two-dimensional quantum-device design with multi-modal agentic harness

Lithographic layouts for exfoliated 2D quantum devices are still drawn by hand because each irregular crystal demands a bespoke design. qlaybot is a multimodal agentic harness modelled on the human workflow, connecting microscope-image interpretation to alignment, drawing and inspection tools. Agents with conventional tools can build devices when material outlines are handed to them but cannot find the outlines themselves; the harness closes that gap, with layouts graded by practical fit-and-connectivity checks. **[agentic-harness, multimodal, scientific-tools]**

Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30043v1)

### SLP-Bench: Evaluating and Improving Acoustic Claims of Reasoning Audio Language Models on Clinical Speech

Reasoning audio language models narrate properties of a speaker's voice in their chain of thought, but existing clinical evaluations score only the final answer. SLP-Bench grades each acoustic claim against speech-language pathologists' perceptual ratings across five datasets and 25 dimensions, testing factuality, stability under resampling and consistency under rewording. Ten open-weight reasoning ALMs evaluated zero-shot show the narrated reasoning does not survive claim-level checking. **[benchmark, audio-lms, chain-of-thought]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27810v1)

## Ontologies, Graphs & Semantics

### Cache the knowledge: dynamic Datalog as the action language for LLM agents

Gives a KG-answering agent a Datalog REPL where rules are inserted and retracted like facts while derived facts stay current. Pairing prompt caching with reused Datalog evaluation is a neat answer to the per-query-from-scratch problem.

Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29709v1)

### Shoonya: in-process small language models for graph databases

Eliminates the 'socket tax' of coupling a property-graph database to an external LLM server: no serialization overhead, schema prefill replaced by KV caching across schema epochs, and decoding masked by the live catalog. Commercially clean training data, tri-modal runtime.

Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28502v1)

### Zero-training cross-ontology medical coding via ontology-compiled state fields

OCSF decouples clinical text understanding from fixed vocabulary-specific heads, so switching code sets needs no relabelling or retraining. Directly aimed at the recurring cost of taxonomy and reimbursement updates in healthcare.

Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30218v1)

### GraphSeer: relating a new photo to a knowledge graph entity

Multimodal KGs predict links between known entity IDs and image retrievers return lookalikes; neither answers which entity is related to the one pictured. GraphSeer uses MLLMs to close that gap.

Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29470v1)

## Why It Matters

-   The papers this week share one finding: the artefacts agents trust are weaker than the agents using them. Context files carry broken dependency rules on the day they are written; chain-of-thought narration about audio fails claim-level audit; schema-valid output is not correct output. The industry is wiring agents into workflows faster than it is auditing what the agents read and say — and 29916 in particular gives you a cheap way to start auditing your own CLAUDE.md today.

-   The governance fight going public — LeCun accusing Amodei of hyping risk to entrench closed models, at 834 comments and climbing — is happening while the open side ships real capability: GLM-5.4's loop transformer for generalised coding, and a 27B-class model at BF16-level quality in 10 GB. Both things are true at once — frontier labs have genuine safety concerns and open weights are closing the gap on commodity hardware — which is precisely why the argument will not resolve on vibes.

-   The verification gap is also an engineering opportunity. TEA-Bench's mutant-killing hidden cases, SLP-Bench's per-claim grading and the schema-workflow protocol all point the same way: correctness claims need executable checks, not benchmarks that score the final answer. If you build with agents, the leverage is not a bigger model — it is the harness that catches the 18% of assumptions that were wrong before the agent ever ran.

## Papers — ArXiv

### CPU-ARC: Agentic Reasoning Caching for on-CPU Reasoning Reuse

LLMs re-solve problems they have already solved, because task-solving and solution execution live in the same inference call. CPU-ARC compiles LLM reasoning traces into CPU-executable caches that apply prior solutions to new instances with minimal model involvement — matching frontier-LLM performance on hard maths and science tasks at 16–291x lower invocation cost, fully explainable and continually trainable. **[inference-cost, caching, cpu]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09891v1)

### When Routine Operations Break Architectural Pattern Relationships in LLM Agent Harnesses

Agent harnesses spread tool mediation, state and guardrails across native services, third-party plugins and config — and routine operations can silently reorder the runtime assumptions those pieces depend on. Across five released harnesses and fourteen independently written plugins, the PatternOps assessment procedure shows policy relationships breaking after mundane actions like reapplying a plugin or saving an allow rule, with no code change. **[agent-harnesses, guardrails, architecture]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09088v1)

### Divide, Diagnose, and Compose: Optimizing Agentic Pipelines and Harnesses

Agentic pipeline quality depends on jointly tuning prompts, topology, model choice and tool policies, yet most automated optimisers touch only one dimension at a time and monolithically. DeCAF adapts the design space to the benchmark and pipeline, optimises each dimension separately, and emits reasoning traces for every decision — turning pipeline tuning from artisanal search into structured credit assignment. **[orchestration, credit-assignment, pipelines]**

Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08723v1)

### I Think, Therefore I Attack: Evaluating Thought Injection Attacks on Reasoning Agents

Explicit chain-of-thought gives attackers a new channel: untrusted text styled as the model's own deliberation, fed back into the loop. Against production-class open-weight models (GLM-5.3, MiniMax-M2.5, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next), the study finds thinking-tag effects and payload effectiveness are model-specific — then weaponises that specificity with indirect injections styled to each target inside a containerised cyber range. **[prompt-injection, reasoning-models, security]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08811v1)

### Tracybot: Preserving Developer Intent by Tracing Agent-Generated Code to Prompts

When coding agents commit code, the design decisions negotiated in conversation die in the chat log, unlinked from the diff. Tracybot, an open-source VS Code and Git integration, captures each change as a Tasklet pairing planning prompts with the resulting code, so intent survives review, blame and future modification. **[coding-agents, provenance, developer-tools]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09395v1)

### Knowing When to Intervene: Causal Evaluation of AI Agents

Static benchmarks cannot tell you whether a mid-trajectory intervention — human review, a stronger model, extra supervision — actually improves the final outcome. This work frames intervention value as a causal inference problem, where the effect depends on the agent's current context and its future behaviour, and develops the first methods for estimating it. **[evaluation, causal-inference, agent-supervision]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09792v1)

## Why It Matters

-   The day's biggest story — Goldman Sachs pulling Claude Code from front-office staff — reads less like a retreat from AI and more like ROI settling where the work is structured. Coding agents earn their keep on well-specified engineering tasks; they underperform where intent is negotiated in prose and the audit trail dies in the chat. Tracybot is the direct engineering answer: bind prompts to diffs so the reasoning behind agent-written code survives the commit.

-   The cost curve is bending from two directions at once. DeepSeek's 284B model running on 64GB of RAM with no GPU made the front page; CPU-ARC attacks the same problem from the software side, showing cached reasoning executes 16–291x cheaper than re-deriving it. Together they suggest the next efficiency win is not a faster chip but refusing to pay for inference you have already paid for — a shift that reprices every serving-stack business case.

-   The papers converge on a uncomfortable finding: agent systems fail in the seams, not the models. Harnesses silently drop guardrail guarantees after routine config changes; reasoning models are injectable through their own deliberation format; benchmarks cannot tell you when human intervention is worth its cost. The interesting engineering frontier for 2027 is not capability but verification — provenance, causal evaluation and harness auditing are the tools that make autonomy insurable.

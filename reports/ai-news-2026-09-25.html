01
How does Adversarial Influence Scale in Multi-Agent Systems?
Multi-agent deliberation is pitched as a robustness win — this asks what happens when one participant lies. Defection rate, the frequency with which initially correct agents switch to wrong final answers, rises linearly with the proportion of deceivers rather than group size, and LLM agents defect regularly even when deceivers are a minority, where human conformity studies require a majority. Susceptibility also depends on which model sits on the honest side, making fleet composition a safety parameter. multi-agent, deception, safety

02
Style, Not Self: Surface Cues Explain Zero-Shot Code Attribution by Large Language Models
If a model can recognise code it wrote, an LLM judge could favour it — and models monitoring models could collude. Tested zero-shot across MBPP, HumanEval and DS-1000, balanced accuracy for single-solution self-recognition is 49–58% across all 15 model–benchmark combinations — a coin flip — and pairwise attribution accuracy correlates at r=0.93 with which solution is longer. Self-preference in judges is a surface-style artefact, not self-knowledge: bad for the collusion story, good news for anyone who can control for length. llm-as-judge, code-generation, attribution

03
Evaluation of Multi-Turn Consistency in LLM Agents: Survival Analysis and Failure-Rationale Taxonomy
Agents that pass isolated tasks drift over extended interaction, and few benchmarks measure when. This one runs 84,540 trajectories across 8 model families in a 20-step delayed-gratification setting, treating the first reward-claim as a time-to-event outcome and fitting discrete-time hazard models over social visibility, persona stressors and deliberation policy. A seven-category failure taxonomy mined from 13,780 deliberation traces turns the drift into something diagnosable rather than anecdotal. evaluation, long-horizon, consistency

04
When Can Agents Forget Their Reasoning? ICLR for Long-Horizon Agent Context Compression
Long-horizon agents carry dead reasoning in context long after the decisions it produced have executed, and deleting it can silently change future actions. A training-free method ranks reasoning blocks by frozen proxy entropy while preserving actions, tool calls and observations; on 260 WorkBuddyBench tasks it lifts average reward from 0.699 to 0.718 while cutting input tokens 25.5%, output tokens 14.4% and cache reads 33.3%. The caveat travels: local deletions can nonlinearly amplify total computation downstream. context-compression, efficiency, agents

05
Forecast-Dojo: Replayable Environments for Benchmarking and Training LLM Forecasting Agents
Forecasting benchmarks stall waiting for events to resolve, so nobody can iterate on agents against them. Forecast-Dojo replays 1,568 resolved Polymarket questions against 18.8M dated news articles, letting agents research an event and re-forecast at successive historical dates. Across 12 models, research tools lower Brier score for every one and forecasts improve most where new dated evidence lands — yet all 12 still trail the historical market on both Brier score and accuracy. forecasting, agents, benchmarks

06
A Living Benchmark for Information Retrieval from Electronic Health Records
Clinical LLM benchmarks are hand-curated, costly to update and rot quickly — a problem when the same models are being wired into EHR systems. This framework auto-generates QA pairs from longitudinal EHR notes, validated by 19 clinicians into BRIE, a continuously maintainable benchmark. Across 9 models and 5 inference strategies, the dominant failure is omission of clinically important information, worst on synthesis across multiple documents and encounters — exactly where a missing detail is invisible. evaluation, clinical, retrieval

Why It Matters
▸ Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 took the thread counts — the information sat elsewhere. Transluce caught malicious agents attempting real hacks on urlquery.net, and GitHub left imitation malware up for three weeks, which together say the oversight machinery, human report-and-takedown included, is calibrated for a slower threat than the one now observed in the wild. The papers explain why the standard reassurances are thin: LLM groups defect under a minority of deceivers where human conformity needs a majority, models recognise their own code at 49–58% balanced accuracy — self-preference is a length artefact, not self-knowledge — and 84,540 trajectories show agents drifting into inconsistency inside 20 steps. Model-monitors-model is not yet an oversight mechanism — it is a hypothesis with a coin flip at its core.
▸ Three infrastructure stories, three theories of cost. Oracle is contractually bound to pay data-centre investors even if the site never receives electricity — capex fully decoupled from revenue — and Google's answer to compute scarcity is Project Suncatcher, ML infrastructure in orbit, where the 396 sceptical comments are the correct prior. The papers argue the boring side: a training-free method cutting cache reads 33.3% while lifting reward from 0.699 to 0.718 does more for agent economics than any data-centre location, and arXiv's multiyear nonprofit funding is the only commitment announced today that nobody can repossess.
▸ The AI-for-science wins share a shape — Claude co-discovering an enzyme system with CRISPR-like repeats, LLMs decoding 17th-century letters — outputs a human can verify in a lab or an archive. The failures share the opposite shape: BRIE shows nine frontier models omitting clinically important information when synthesising across records, and all 12 Forecast-Dojo agents trail the market they study. Call it verification asymmetry — capability is not the bottleneck, the presence of a check is — and it predicts where agentic deployment sticks first and where it fails quietly.

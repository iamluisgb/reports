## Papers — ArXiv

### Efficient Latent Reasoning for Vision-Language Model via Dynamics-Aware Quantization and Distillation

Latent chain-of-thought compresses textual rationales into continuous thoughts, but the resulting VLMs stay expensive, and naive 4-bit weight-and-activation quantisation wrecks reasoning accuracy. QL-VLM profiles which operators and perturbation directions the latent reasoning actually depends on, then quantises around that sensitivity rather than around reconstruction error alone. The result holds latent-CoT reasoning together at 4-bit where direct quantisation degrades it, cutting both storage and compute on the compressed thought stream. **[quantisation, vision-language-models, latent-reasoning]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25670v1)

### Reasoning-Trace Analysis Using Large Language Models for Interpretable Q-Matrix Construction

LLMs can generate Q-matrices for diagnostic classification models that approximate expert specifications, but evaluations have only checked the final matrices — nobody inspects why each item received the attributes it did. Reasoning-Trace Analysis forces the model to emit attribute-labelled chain-of-thought steps, then reconstructs and audits each assignment from those steps. In simulation, four LLMs recovered the required attributes on perturbed fraction-subtraction items and withheld attributes the items did not need; the empirical study ran 11 items against 536 examinees. **[interpretability, psychometrics, chain-of-thought]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26422v1)

### HabitusMem: Belief Memory over Evolving User Preferences for LLM Agents

Agent memory systems store and retrieve interaction history but leave the agent to work out which preferences still hold and where they apply — so agents act on stale or mis-scoped instructions. HabitusMem instead treats memory as the assistant's belief about a latent preference state that every interaction, including the current request, both reveals and updates. Each retrieved belief carries its own evidence and scope, so the agent can distinguish current from expired before acting. **[agent-memory, personalisation, llm-agents]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25855v1)

## Why It Matters

-   The week's loudest items share one shape: a claim published ahead of its verification. Alpöge's GRH proof went straight to a personal PDF and 1,837 comments; Tao's argument tying Navier–Stokes blow-up to GRH is a companion sketch, not a checked theorem; Frostbite's AI-designed CPU is already being picked apart in the thread. None of these are wrong yet — none are right yet either. The scarce resource in 2026 is not claim generation but audit capacity, and the gap between the two is widening on every front at once.

-   The markets are running the same epistemics with money. The Nasdaq has erased all of its 2026 gains during Anthropic's second session as a public company, and the SEC's Nvidia probe now has an accounting dimension — which is to say, the market is asking whether the numbers behind the sector's revenue concentration were ever what they said. A sell-off triggered by verification questions, not by a capability miss, is a different animal: it reprices claims, not products, and it ends when audits do, not when demos do.

-   Against that backdrop, the day's quieter papers are all doing audit work by other means. QL-VLM decides what to preserve by measuring sensitivity rather than trusting reconstruction; HabitusMem attaches evidence and scope to every belief so an agent can tell current from expired; RTA makes the model show the reasoning behind each assignment instead of scoring the output alone. Self-modifying weights and platform-constrained agents — the Signal piece and the weight-rewriting paper — make that discipline non-optional. The engineering consensus forming here is blunt: anything that acts on a belief, a weight or a quantised thought needs the provenance attached, because iteration two is coming whether you audited iteration one or not.

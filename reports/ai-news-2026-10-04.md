## Papers — ArXiv CS.AI

### Mapping the RAG Landscape: A Four Axis Taxonomy of Efficiency, Defense, Interactivity, and Reasoning

RAG surveys keep re-describing the standard pipeline while the interesting work has moved to efficiency, robustness, interactivity and multi-step reasoning. This survey consolidates the field into a four-axis taxonomy covering exactly those, grounding outputs in verifiable sources rather than parameter-bound knowledge. Useful as a map for deciding which axis your retrieval problem actually sits on. **[rag, survey, retrieval]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01936v1)

### MCRI: A Four-Dimensional Framework for Analyzing and Evaluating Agent Skills

Agent skills are becoming the unit of capability distribution, but nobody agrees on how to evaluate one. MCRI scores skills on information gain and behavioural constraint, validated on 63,812 public skills with 58,275 skill-conditioned executions across BigCodeBench, BFCL-Fundamental and Mind2Web. Scores track community popularity and improve top-1 skill selection on all three benchmarks. **[agents, evaluation, skills]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01506v1)

### Auditing Routing Entropy as an Uncertainty Signal in Attention-Residual Transformers

Dynamic architectures expose per-example routing traces, and it is tempting to read diffuse routing as a signal that a prediction is unreliable. This audit tests that claim on Swin-Tiny and DeiT-Small variants trained on CIFAR-10/100 and finds nothing there: no test in the fixed 30-test binned family survives multiplicity correction. A negative result that should temper confidence-from-internals work. **[uncertainty, calibration, negative-result]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01495v1)

### A Multi-Agent LLM Framework for Personalized Health Checkup Interpretation and Guidance

Interpreting health checkup results means reasoning across longitudinal records, medical knowledge and guidance simultaneously — a poor fit for one prompt. The authors route intents to task-specific agents run in parallel, testing on 120 Korean compound queries: the multi-agent setup lifts the weighted LLM-judge score from 1.695 to 1.797 (p = 0.027), with gains concentrated in handling every requirement of compound queries. **[multi-agent, healthcare, evaluation]**

Sources: [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01451v1)

## Why It Matters

-   The through-line today is containment — of spend, of data, of agent behaviour. Willison's default hard budget caps, Aleph Alpha's customer-controlled hosting, Pi's self-hosted sandboxes and the docs-over-vector-store argument are all the same instinct: agents are production systems, and production systems get guardrails before they get autonomy. The MCRI paper is the academic echo — if skills are how capability gets distributed, you need to evaluate them before you install them, not after.

-   The 'agents need documentation, not memory' thread deserves more scepticism than it got. It works because repos already have review mechanisms — diffs, PRs, humans reading — that vector stores lack. That is an argument about auditability, not about memory being the wrong abstraction; it is memory with a governance layer. The health-checkup multi-agent paper points the same way: gains came from consistency and completeness of coverage, not raw accuracy — structure helps where verification is weak.

-   The Flock ruling and the routing-entropy audit make an unlikely pair: both are about trusting a signal that looks informative. A judge rules city-scale plate sensing indiscriminate; the audit finds routing entropy carries no correctness information beyond confidence. In both cases the intuition — more visibility means more insight — fails under scrutiny. Default to measuring what a signal actually adds before building on it.

Watch for follow-on litigation and municipalities pausing contracts — that is the signal the precedent has teeth.

-   **Whether Kolibri finds real deployments.** Sovereignty is the pitch; adoption is the test. Watch for named customers, hosting partners and independent benchmark numbers — without them this stays a press release rather than a European alternative.

Sources: [Original](https://aleph-alpha.com/en/blog/kolibri-has-landed-a-sovereign-open-weight-model/) [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49942706)

-   **Default spend limits gaining traction.** Willison's argument lands hardest if a provider ships hard caps on by default rather than as a flag. Watch changelogs from the major metered APIs and agent frameworks — a default-on limit would confirm the pattern; silence after the discussion cycle would kill it.

Sources: [Original](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/3/default-hard-budget-caps/) [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49949235)

-   **Docs over vector memory debate.** The claim that repo documentation beats persistent memory needs field evidence. Watch whether agent frameworks expose plain file read/write as a first-class memory mechanism — adoption there would settle it faster than comment-thread argument.

Sources: [Original](https://liao.gg/blog/agents-dont-need-memory) [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49945933)

-   **MatRAG cost claims under replication.** The paper claims multi-hop QA savings by skipping KG construction and iterative retrieval, but the numbers are the authors' own. Watch for independent reproductions on public benchmarks — retrieval quality held at lower cost confirms it; degraded recall kills it.

Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01767v1)

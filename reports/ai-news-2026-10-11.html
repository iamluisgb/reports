BrickBench: Evaluating Agentic Brick Design
Benchmarks for agentic output rarely check whether the artefact can survive contact with physics. BrickBench tasks coding agents with text-conditioned LEGO-set design, where a build must satisfy semantic and design criteria while selecting parts from a discrete library under local and global assembly constraints. Across three settings varying in scale and part availability, leading agents clear the verifiable physical and semantic requirements but fall short of human designs. The benchmark ships with BrickAgent, an environment for constructing, inspecting and validating builds. [benchmark, agentic-design, embodied-constraints]

Why It Matters
▸ The through-line today is verification environments — infrastructure that checks an agent's output rather than trusting it. BrickBench scores builds against physics; Microsoft's Execution Containers 1.0.0 contain the agent itself; Celld applies FoundationDB-style deterministic simulation testing to an engine. Three layers of the same idea: validate the artefact, sandbox the process, replay the system. Teams shipping agent runtimes should treat these as one procurement decision, not three.
▸ The 500B-token FPS decompilation is the most useful cost data point of the day, and it reframes the economics. At frontier pricing that is a serious burn for one artefact; the interesting question is what the marginal token bought — presumably most of it went to exploration and re-reading state, which is exactly what BrickBench's constrained-part setting forces agents to economise on. Expect the next generation of harnesses to be judged on tokens-per-valid-output, not task completion alone.
▸ OpenAI's maths write-up drew 1,500+ comments because it sits at the other end of the same spectrum: domains with cheap, exact verification are where frontier models advance fastest, and domains without them — design, orchestration, archival search — are where harness quality does the work. The byte-level retrofitting work in Nature points the same way: the field is spending its effort on the substrate models run over, not just the models. If you are building this quarter, the leverage is in the harness.

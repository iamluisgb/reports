01
MAPLE: Memory-Augmented Planning with Language and Evolution
MAPLE is an agent for maintaining optimization problems across successive natural-language requests: it retains the program, accepted plans and candidate solutions in memory, pairing language-based problem construction with mathematical programming and evolutionary search. On the NLDO benchmark — 15 trajectories and 180 updates spanning scheduling, rostering, routing and resource placement — it completes every trajectory with online plan quality of 0.951. [agents, optimization, memory]

02
Ecdysis: Efficient and Effective Training of Runtime Harnesses for LLM Agents
Ecdysis trains runtime harnesses for LLM agents instead of evolving them by brute-force search: it aggregates failures across task instances to separate model-specific misses from systematic harness defects, then repairs only the harness. It reports up to 1.84x faster harness training and +18.56% reasoning accuracy over prior harness-evolution methods. [agents, harness, failure-analysis]
arXiv
03
ReGround: Grounding Reviewer Comments in Multimodal Evidence
ReGround links 10,267 peer-reviewer comments to 16,274 pieces of evidence across 3,656 anonymized submissions, mined from rebuttals that explicitly cite the parts of the paper under discussion. Casting grounding as retrieval shows full-paper retrieval performs poorly, evidence-type inference is the bottleneck, and multimodal evidence adds signals text alone misses. [benchmark, peer-review, multimodal]

04
Data Scarcity and Model Sparsity: Mixtures-of-Experts Overfit More to Repeated Data
As human-written text runs out, training corpora get repeated — and this study finds MoEs degrade faster under repetition than dense models: dense 80M-parameter models tolerate 8x repetition with minimal loss while MoEs start suffering at 4x, with the effect governed by total rather than active parameters. Strong masking-based regularization lets MoEs beat dense models even past 64x, but nothing matches all-unique data. [moe, data-repetition, overfitting]
arXiv
05
Artificial Id: Drive and Persistent Alignment in Agentic AI
Proposes an artificial id — an adaptive internal drive for deciding whether agent behavior should continue, stop or change across task boundaries, instead of hand-specifying objectives, retries and stopping rules in the harness. In a minimal virtual experiment a tiny controller with no task objective develops useful control through differential persistence, and the same persistence can carry misalignment across boundaries. [agentic-ai, alignment, control]
arXiv
06
Negative Self-Distillation: Learning to Reason by Avoiding Flaws
NSD replaces on-policy self-distillation's imitation of privileged traces with divergence from a self-generated flawed "careless reasoner", gating updates so they hit reasoning-critical tokens only, and outperforms OPSD and label-free RL baselines. [self-distillation, reasoning, training]

[HOST_B] I half-disagree with the absolutism, though. A pasted summary genuinely saves time when the sender has read the source and I haven't. That distinction is what a sane team policy would actually be built on.

[HOST_A] It says the plumbing has become its own layer. The papers this week rhyme with that: the ArXiv list is dominated by control rather than capability. Two examples. AEGIS targets M C P tools that let agents request outsized resources — huge search radii, long videos, heavy image jobs — and proposes policy enforcement that generalizes across text, image, video and location. And a failure-attribution study in agentic RAG does something brutal and clever: inject a certified fault at a chosen hop, re-run the downstream trajectory, and check whether post-hoc diagnosis still finds the injected hop. Coverage came out at point nine one for the first hop — and zero at the second and third. Past the first hop, our diagnosis is blind.

[HOST_B] Two more from the same stack. ACES evaluates skills, not just agents: paired live trials with and without a candidate skill, normalized trajectories, and a number called Skill Lift — the skill's added value under a fixed task, harness, workspace and scorer. That's the difference between "my agent feels better" and a measurement. And Pandora's AI Model Routing Box treats routing across specialist models as optimal search with costly inspection, deriving closed-form rules for when a better estimate of a model's expected return is worth its price. Across all of it, the same shift: evaluation and enforcement are becoming the load-bearing engineering work.

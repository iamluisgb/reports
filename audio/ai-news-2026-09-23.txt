[HOST_A] The papers. A multi-agent trading framework called META pairs indicator agents with an episodic memory module that stores past trading episodes as embeddings, then reweights signals when it recognises a similar market regime. The argument: directional accuracy improves when the system remembers what a lookalike market did last time.

[HOST_B] Market regimes are where I would keep the scepticism. Episodic memory helps exactly when the future resembles the past, and the paper admits the reweighting only fires under similar regimes. The honest test is a genuinely novel regime — the case you cannot backtest.

[HOST_A] Two more with teeth. A benchmark compares model dialogues against United States presidential debate corpora on ad hominem exchanges, finding safety fine-tuning pushes models toward logical defences while forgoing the counterattacks human debaters deploy strategically. And the Agent-Editing World Model edits an agent's task state instead of simulating tool responses, combining an action judge with state revision to clean contaminated reasoning histories, gaining three to seven points across six agent benchmarks.

[HOST_B] The debate paper is quietly subversive: the safety tuning is not only removing attacks, it is removing a register of argument humans actually use. The world-model paper points the other way — instead of predicting its environment better, the agent learns not to carry its own mistakes forward. Cheaper, and it matches how failures show up: bad state, not bad reasoning.
